Most roadmaps fail because nobody agreed on what ‘done’ means

Three questions to ask before anything goes on the board, and the one that caused more arguments than it settled.

By Maya Fernandes, Head of Product, FintechMUMBAI · 26 SEP 2026 · 8 MIN READ

Most roadmaps fail not because of bad prioritisation but because nobody agreed on what done means. The engineering lead hears a shipped feature, the designer hears a tested one, and sales hears something they can put in a proposal on Monday.

The first question I now ask is about the customer, not the team. What will a customer be able to do that they couldn't do the week before? If nobody can answer in one sentence, the item isn't ready for the board.

The second is about evidence. How will we know, four weeks after release, whether it worked? Pick the measure before the work starts, and write down the number that would make you stop.

The third is about ownership. Who decides the item is finished? One name, not a team. Shared ownership felt fair, but in practice it meant nobody could close anything.

The question I dropped was about effort. Estimating size at the roadmap stage invited debates that belonged in sprint planning, and it anchored everyone on numbers we never revisited.
